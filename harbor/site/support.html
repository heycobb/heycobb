Support

Need a hand? Email support@ownhold.app.

It helps to include your device and its software version, your NAS model and DSM version, how you're connecting (home Wi-Fi, Tailscale or the internet), and the exact message you see. Please never send your password.

What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad with iOS / iPadOS 26 or later, or a Mac with macOS 26 or later.
  • A Synology NAS running DSM 7, with HTTPS turned on. Port 5001 is the standard setting.
  • A DSM account that's allowed to use File Station.
  • Or another NAS (QNAP, UGREEN, UniFi, TrueNAS and others) with file sharing (SMB) or WebDAV turned on. See Other NAS.
  • For access away from home (optional): Tailscale or another VPN, Synology DDNS, or your QuickConnect ID. See below.

Want to look around first? Tap Try the Demo Library on the welcome screen. It works without a NAS.

Getting started

Ownhold has a step-by-step guide built in, written for people who've never connected to a NAS from their phone. Open it from the welcome screen, from the Add NAS screen, or from Settings › Setup Guide. In short:

  1. Find your NAS's address on your home network, for example 192.168.1.20.
  2. Check HTTPS is on in DSM: Control Panel › Login Portal › DSM.
  3. In Ownhold, tap Add a NAS, enter the address, your account and password.
  4. The first time, you'll be asked to check the NAS's certificate. That's normal; see below.

Other NAS

Enter your NAS's name or address and your NAS account. Ownhold checks whether it's a Synology; if not, it connects with file sharing (SMB), which every NAS offers. NAS that announce file sharing on your network also appear in the setup screen.

  • SMB isn't encrypted on most NAS, so use it at home or over a VPN such as Tailscale, which encrypts everything.
  • WebDAV works over HTTPS from anywhere your NAS can be reached. Turn it on in your NAS's settings, then enter its address with the HTTPS port, for example nas.local:5006, or the full address if it has a path.
  • Browsing, opening, uploading, offline files, Photo Backup and the Files app work the same. Thumbnails are made on your device. Share links, extracting archives and Recently Deleted need a Synology; Compress zips on your device and uploads the zip.

Where is Settings?

On iPhone and iPad, tap the server button at the top of the list of shared folders, then Settings. On a Mac, choose Ownhold › Settings… (⌘,).

Using it away from home

Ownhold works with Tailscale, a free, private network between your own devices, with nothing opened on your router.

  1. On the NAS: in DSM, open Package Center, install Tailscale, open it and sign in.
  2. On your iPhone, iPad or Mac: install the Tailscale app, sign in with the same account and switch it on.
  3. In the Tailscale app, find your NAS. Its name looks like nas.tailnet-name.ts.net, or use its address that starts with 100.
  4. In Ownhold, open Settings, tap your NAS, and enter that name as the Private network (VPN) address.

Keep Tailscale switched on when you're out. Ownhold uses your home address when you're home and Tailscale everywhere else, automatically.

Other ways to connect

  • Another VPN: ZeroTier, NetBird, WireGuard (DSM's VPN Server package), Headscale and similar all work while their app is running. Enter the NAS's address on that network as the Private network (VPN) address.
  • Synology DDNS (yourname.synology.me): set it up in DSM under Control Panel › External Access › DDNS, forward port 5001 on your router, add a free Let's Encrypt certificate (Control Panel › Security › Certificate), and enter the name as the Internet address. This makes DSM reachable from the internet, so turn on 2-factor sign-in and auto-block.
  • QuickConnect ID: find it in DSM under Control Panel › External Access › QuickConnect and enter it when you add your NAS. Ownhold asks Synology for your NAS's current addresses, then connects directly. It never uses Synology's relay servers, so away from home it works only when your NAS can be reached directly (for example with port forwarding). Otherwise, use a VPN.

The “Check Certificate” screen

Most home NAS boxes use their own certificate rather than one from a public authority, so the first time you connect, Ownhold shows its fingerprint and asks you to trust it. If you set up the NAS yourself and you're at home, tap Trust. Ownhold remembers the certificate for all of that NAS's addresses.

To double-check, open your NAS's address in Safari on a Mac at home (for example https://192.168.1.20:5001), choose Show Details › view the certificate, and compare the SHA-256 fingerprint.

If Ownhold later says the certificate changed and you didn't replace it in DSM, don't continue. Something may be intercepting the connection.

Uploading and organising

  • Upload: open a folder, tap +, then Upload Photos and Videos or Upload Files…. Photos keep their original format (for example HEIC). On a Mac or iPad you can also drag files or folders onto the list.
  • If a file with the same name is already there, Ownhold asks whether to Keep Both (the new one gets a number), Replace it, or Skip it.
  • Uploads run while Ownhold is open. On iPhone and iPad the screen stays on until they finish. Tap Details on the upload bar to see each file, cancel, or retry ones that failed.
  • New folder: tap +, then New Folder.
  • Rename, move or delete: touch and hold an item (right-click on a Mac). To work on several at once, open the view menu (the lines at the top right) and choose Select.
  • Deleting removes the item from your NAS. If the Recycle Bin is turned on for that shared folder in DSM (Control Panel › Shared Folder › Edit), you can restore it from there.
  • On a Mac (or an iPad with a keyboard), the menu bar has the same commands: File › New Folder (⇧⌘N) and Upload Files… (⇧⌘U), View › Select Items, then File › Rename… or Delete… (⌘⌫) for what you've selected.
  • Shared folders themselves can only be created, renamed or removed in DSM.
  • Compress and extract: choose Compress in an item's menu (or with several selected) to make a zip next to it. If your NAS doesn't let apps compress, Ownhold zips the items on your device and uploads the zip instead, which takes longer for big folders. Extract Here unpacks an archive on the NAS: a single folder lands next to the archive, anything else goes into a new folder named after it. Nothing already there is replaced. Password-protected archives have to be extracted in DSM.
  • Favorites: choose Add to Favorites in a folder's menu to pin it to the sidebar.
  • Recents: files you've opened are listed under Recents in the sidebar. Tap Clear to empty it, or turn off Settings › Show Recents.

Sharing

  • Share… sends a copy from your device with any app. A folder arrives as a folder, so Save to Files keeps it intact (very large folders come as a zip).
  • Share Link… asks your NAS for a link that anyone who has it can open, optionally with a password and an expiry date. Stop Sharing turns the link off. You can also see and remove links in DSM's File Station under Shared Links Manager. Your account needs permission to share in DSM.

Video and music

  • Videos and music stream straight from your NAS. MP4, MOV, M4V, MP3, AAC, M4A, WAV, AIFF and FLAC play directly; other formats open after downloading.
  • Music keeps playing with the screen locked or in another app, with controls on the Lock Screen and in Control Center, until you close the player. It goes on to the next track in the folder.
  • Videos can float in Picture in Picture while you use other apps.
  • With Settings › Remember Playback Position on, both pick up where you left off.

Photo Backup

  • Open Photo Backup in the sidebar, allow access to Photos, and choose a folder on your NAS. Photos and videos are saved there as originals (HEIC stays HEIC), in Year and Month folders, dated when they were taken.
  • Tap Back Up Now. Ownhold remembers what it has backed up, and skips files already in the folder with the same name and size, so nothing is uploaded twice.
  • Back Up Automatically (off unless you turn it on) also backs up when you open Ownhold, and in the background when iOS allows. By default only on Wi-Fi and while charging, and never in Low Power Mode.
  • Photos kept in iCloud are downloaded first, which uses data.
  • On a Mac, Back Up Automatically runs when new photos arrive in your library, while Ownhold is running.
  • Using iCloud Photos on more than one device? Back up from all of them to the same folder: a photo already backed up from your iPhone is skipped on your Mac, and the other way round.

Files app and Finder

  • Turn on Settings › Show NAS in Files App (Show NAS in Finder on a Mac). Your NAS then appears under Browse › Locations in the Files app, and in Finder's sidebar on a Mac. The first time, the Files app may ask you to turn Ownhold on.
  • Files download only when you open them. New folders, uploads, renames, moves and deletes made there go straight to your NAS, and changes you make in Ownhold show up there within a few seconds.
  • While Ownhold is open, it checks every few minutes for changes made elsewhere (on another device or in DSM) in folders you opened recently there, so they show up without reopening the folder. To turn this off, turn off Settings › Keep Finder Up to Date (Keep Files App Up to Date on iPhone and iPad). It's skipped in Low Power Mode.
  • On a Mac, right-click a folder or file in Finder and choose Keep Downloaded to keep it on your Mac, so it opens instantly and works offline. New files on the NAS in that folder download automatically while Ownhold is open. Choose Stop Keeping Downloaded to undo it; Finder's Remove Download then frees the space.
  • It's off while the app lock is on, because the Files app and Finder can't ask for Face ID or Touch ID.
  • Turning it off removes the location and deletes the copies the system kept. On iPhone and iPad, an empty Ownhold entry stays in Files; tap ⋯ › Edit in Files to hide it.

Folder Backup (Mac)

  • Open Folder Backup in the sidebar, add the Mac folders you want to keep safe (for example Documents or Desktop), and choose a folder on your NAS. Each Mac folder is copied into a folder of the same name there, with everything inside it.
  • Choose Back Up Now, or turn on Back Up When Files Change to copy new and changed files about a minute after they change, while Ownhold is running (the menu bar keeps it running with the window closed). It never runs in Low Power Mode.
  • Files are only added or updated on your NAS, never deleted: deleting a file on your Mac leaves its backup on the NAS. Hidden files and temporary files are skipped.

Encrypted folders

  • In any folder, tap + and choose New Encrypted Folder (Ownhold Pro). Choose a name and a password. Files you put in it are encrypted on your device before they're uploaded, so your NAS only ever stores scrambled data.
  • You'll see a recovery key once. Keep it somewhere safe, such as your password manager. If you forget the password, it's the only way back in: no one else can open the files, including us.
  • To open an encrypted folder on another device, open it in Ownhold and enter the password. Under Next Time, choose how it opens on that device from then on:
    • Ask for Password: the password each time.
    • Use Face ID (or Touch ID): Face ID, with your device passcode if Face ID doesn't work. If a face or fingerprint is added on the device later, the password is needed once.
    • Keep Unlocked: no prompt. Only this option shows the folder in the Files app or Finder.
  • Tap the open lock at the top to lock the folder, or touch and hold it to change Next Time. Folders that aren't kept unlocked lock when Ownhold quits or after 3 minutes away.
  • To encrypt files you already have, select them (or touch and hold one), then choose Encrypt into New Folder (under More when selecting). Ownhold makes the encrypted folder and moves them into it.
  • Moving files in or out of an encrypted folder encrypts or decrypts them. The original is deleted only once the copy is complete.
  • Your NAS can't read the files, so share links, extracting archives on the NAS and opening the files in other NAS apps (such as Synology Photos) don't work in an encrypted folder. Thumbnails and search work in Ownhold.
  • Forgot the password? Open the folder, choose Forgot Password?, enter the recovery key and set a new password.

Ownhold Pro

Ownhold is free to download. Ownhold Pro is a one-time purchase of $9.99 (or the equivalent where you live) that unlocks it on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and is shared with your family through Family Sharing. There's no subscription, and nothing you can already do is taken away.

Free

  • Connecting to one NAS: Synology, or any NAS over SMB or WebDAV, with setup help for Tailscale and QuickConnect.
  • Browsing, searching, opening and previewing files; uploading, downloading, moving, renaming and deleting.
  • Video and music players, Picture in Picture, share links, Compress and Extract.
  • Files kept offline, Favorites and Recents.
  • Your NAS in the Files app and Finder.
  • Photo Backup with Back Up Now.
  • Save to Ownhold from other apps' share sheets.
  • The app lock and every privacy and security setting.

Pro

  • Back Up Automatically for Photo Backup.
  • Folder Backup on a Mac.
  • Photos: your NAS's photos and videos by month.
  • Keep Downloaded and Keep Finder Up to Date (Keep Files App Up to Date on iPhone and iPad).
  • Shortcuts and Siri actions.
  • Scan to NAS on iPhone and iPad.
  • Large uploads that finish after you leave Ownhold (iPhone and iPad, Synology).
  • More than one NAS.
  • Wake NAS and Recently Deleted.
  • Encrypted folders. Opening, unlocking and moving files out of an encrypted folder always work without Pro, so you can always get to your files.

To buy Pro, open Settings › Ownhold Pro, or tap any item with a lock. Bought it on another device, or reinstalled? Choose Restore Purchases there.

Photos

  • Photos in the sidebar shows every photo and video in the folders you choose, newest first and grouped by month, like the Photos app. On iPhone and iPad it starts with your Photo Backup folder.
  • Add or remove folders with the folder button. The list is kept on your device and refreshes while Photos is open; nothing runs in the background.
  • Photos are sorted by their file dates. Photo Backup sets those to when each photo was taken.

Cellular data

  • In Settings › Cellular Data (Personal Hotspot on a Mac), choose what may use cellular data: automatic Photo Backup (Folder Backup on a Mac), Large Uploads (files over 50 MB) and, on a Mac, Keep Downloaded.
  • When something waits for Wi-Fi, it says so. Things you start yourself, like Back Up Now or opening a file, always run.
  • Low Data Mode pauses automatic backups and downloads.

Wake NAS

  • If your NAS is asleep, choose Wake NAS under Can't Reach NAS, in the NAS menu, or in the Mac menu bar. Ownhold wakes it and connects as soon as it answers.
  • It needs the NAS's MAC address. Ownhold reads it from DSM when you sign in with an administrator account at home; otherwise enter it in Settings › Edit NAS › Wake NAS (DSM: Control Panel › Network › Network Interface).
  • Turn on Wake on LAN in DSM (Control Panel › Hardware & Power). It works only on your home network.

Recently Deleted

  • If a shared folder has DSM's Recycle Bin turned on (Control Panel › Shared Folder › Edit), Recently Deleted appears in the sidebar. Browse it, then choose Restore to put an item back where it was, or Delete Permanently.
  • Your account needs access to the Recycle Bin; DSM can limit it to administrators.

Scan to NAS (iPhone and iPad)

  • In a folder, tap + › Scan Document. Scan the pages, then name the PDF and tap Save. It uploads to that folder; nothing is kept on your device.

Shortcuts and Siri

  • In the Shortcuts app, Ownhold adds Upload Files to NAS, Get Share Link, Open in Ownhold, Wake NAS and Back Up Photos Now (Back Up Folders Now on a Mac).
  • Folders and files to choose from are your shared folders, Favorites and Recents. Add a folder to Favorites to use it in a shortcut.
  • Ask Siri to "Wake my NAS with Ownhold" or "Back up now with Ownhold".
  • On iPhone and iPad, Scan to NAS opens the folder you choose and starts the document scanner.

Save to Ownhold

  • In Photos, Files, Safari downloads, Mail attachments and other apps, tap the share button and choose Ownhold (on a Mac, Share › Ownhold). Pick a shared folder and tap Save Here.
  • It uses the NAS you're signed in to in Ownhold. If your session has expired, open Ownhold and sign in first.
  • If a file with the same name is already there, the new one gets a number. Nothing is overwritten.
  • With the app lock on, Ownhold asks for Face ID or Touch ID first.
  • Don't see Ownhold in the share sheet? Tap More at the end of the app row and turn it on.

Sharing and playing

  • Share several files: choose Select, pick the files, then tap Share. Ownhold downloads them, then opens the share sheet so you can send them by Mail or Messages, save them to Files or Photos, and so on. A folder is sent as a zip file. Tap Cancel on the bar to stop.
  • Videos and music (MP4, MOV, M4V, MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV, AIFF and FLAC) play straight from your NAS without downloading the whole file first. You can skip ahead, change the speed and use AirPlay.
  • Other video formats, such as MKV and AVI, can't be played by Apple's player. Ownhold downloads them so you can open them in another app.
  • Streaming uses the same encrypted, certificate-checked connection as the rest of the app, and nothing is saved on your device.

Keeping files offline

  • On iPhone and iPad, swipe left on a file or folder and tap Offline. Or touch and hold it (right-click on a Mac) and choose Keep Offline.
  • Everything you've saved is under Offline in the sidebar, and works with no connection at all.
  • When Ownhold connects to your NAS, it updates saved files that changed there. It doesn't sync in the background.
  • Saved files are encrypted while your iPhone or iPad is locked, and aren't included in backups.

Appearance

In Settings › Appearance you can choose:

  • System, Light or Dark, for Ownhold only.
  • Accent Color: Evergreen, Forest, Harbor Blue, Midnight, Graphite, Onyx, Rosewood or Espresso, or System to match your Mac's accent color.
  • Sidebar: Tinted with the accent color, or Plain.
  • App Icon: Evergreen, Paper, Graphite or Harbor Blue. On iPhone and iPad this changes the Home Screen icon. On a Mac it changes the Dock icon while Ownhold is open.

Locking the app

In Settings, turn on Lock with Face ID or Passcode (Lock with Touch ID or Password on a Mac). On iPhone and iPad, Hide Files in App Switcher covers your files when you switch apps.

The menu bar (Mac)

Ownhold adds its monogram to the Mac menu bar. From there you can see how you're connected, follow or cancel uploads, upload files to a shared folder, open a shared folder, open recently saved offline files, and switch NAS. When the app is locked, the menu shows no file or folder names. To hide it, turn off Settings › Show Ownhold in Menu Bar. While it's showing, closing the window leaves Ownhold running, so uploads can finish, and Ownhold leaves the Dock until you open it again. To keep the Dock icon, turn off Settings › Hide Dock Icon When Closed.

To have Ownhold start when you log in, turn on Settings › Open at Login. With the menu bar item on, it starts quietly in the menu bar, without opening a window.

Managing your NAS

  • Change addresses or the name: Settings, then tap the NAS.
  • Add another NAS: the server menu, then Add a NAS….
  • Remove a NAS: in Settings, swipe left on it (right-click on a Mac) and choose Remove. This deletes its offline files and sign-in from your device. Nothing on the NAS is affected.

If something doesn't work

Find the message you see below.

Can't reach the NAS

“Can't reach the NAS. Check that you're on your home network, or that your VPN (such as Tailscale) is connected.”

  • At home: check this device is on your home Wi-Fi, and that the address in Settings is right.
  • Away: make sure Tailscale (or your other VPN) is switched on and shows your NAS as connected.
  • Using QuickConnect away from home? It only finds direct routes to your NAS. If your router doesn't forward a port to it, add a VPN address instead.
  • On iPhone and iPad, check Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network allows Ownhold.
  • Your saved offline files keep working in the meantime.
Ownhold only connects over HTTPS

Ownhold never uses plain HTTP, so your password is always encrypted. Remove “http://” from the address, turn on HTTPS in DSM (Control Panel › Login Portal), and use port 5001.

Incorrect account name or password

Use the same account and password you use to sign in to DSM in a web browser. Account names are not the same as display names.

Verification code didn't work

“That verification code didn't work. Try the next one.”

Codes change every 30 seconds. Wait for a new one, and check the time on your phone and NAS is set automatically. Turn on Remember this device so you're not asked again.

Temporarily blocked

“The NAS has temporarily blocked this device after too many failed sign-ins.”

DSM's auto-block protects against password guessing. Wait for it to expire, or remove the block in DSM under Control Panel › Security › Protection.

Account disabled, or password expired

Sign in to DSM in a browser as an administrator: Control Panel › User & Group lets you re-enable an account. If your password expired, change it in DSM, then sign in to Ownhold again.

You don't have permission to open this

Your account needs File Station access, and permission for that shared folder. In DSM: Control Panel › User & Group, select the account, Edit, then check Applications › File Station and Permissions.

Your session expired

DSM ends sessions after a while or when settings change. Sign in again. If you use 2-factor sign-in with “Remember this device”, you won't need a code.

The certificate changed

If you renewed or replaced your NAS's certificate in DSM, trust the new one. If you didn't, don't continue, especially on public Wi-Fi, and check your NAS from home.

An item with that name already exists there

Choose a different name, or move or rename the existing item first. When uploading, Ownhold offers Keep Both or Replace instead.

The NAS is out of space

The volume is full, or your account has a storage quota in DSM (Control Panel › User & Group › Edit › Quota). Free up space or ask the NAS's administrator to raise the quota.

Your account doesn't have permission for this

Uploading, renaming, moving and deleting need Read/Write permission for that shared folder. In DSM: Control Panel › Shared Folder, select it, Edit › Permissions.

This NAS can't stream that file

Your NAS didn't accept a request for part of the file, which streaming needs. Ownhold downloads the whole file instead. If this happens with every video, check that DSM is up to date.

That QuickConnect ID wasn't found

Check the ID in DSM under Control Panel › External Access › QuickConnect, and that QuickConnect is turned on there. IDs use only letters, numbers and hyphens. If the lookup service is briefly unavailable, Ownhold uses your saved addresses instead.

Something else

Email support@ownhold.app with the exact message and what you were doing. Ownhold doesn't collect logs or analytics, so your description is what helps.
